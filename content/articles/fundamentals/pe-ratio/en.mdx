The PE ratio (Price-to-Earnings Ratio) is one of the most common and important metrics used in stock analysis. It measures a company's stock price relative to its earnings per share (EPS).

## How is the PE Ratio Calculated?

**Formula:** Stock Price / Earnings Per Share (EPS)

For example, if a company's stock price is ₹500 and its EPS is ₹25, the PE ratio = 500 / 25 = 20. This means investors are currently willing to pay ₹20 for every ₹1 of earnings.

## Types of PE Ratios

### 1. Trailing PE
Based on the actual earnings of the trailing 12 months. This is based on historical, concrete data.

### 2. Forward PE
Based on estimated earnings for the next 12 months. This factors in expected future earnings growth.

## How to Interpret the PE Ratio

- **Low PE (< 15):** The stock may be undervalued relative to its earnings, OR the company faces challenges.
- **Moderate PE (15-25):** The stock may be fairly valued.
- **High PE (> 25):** The stock may be overvalued, OR high growth is expected.

> **Important:** Never make investment decisions based on PE ratio alone. It's just one piece of the puzzle.

## Caveats to Keep in Mind

1. **Sector Differences:** IT and FMCG sectors typically have higher PEs, while banks and utilities tend to have lower PEs.
2. **Growth Rate:** High-growth companies deserve higher PEs.
3. **Economic Cycle:** PE ratios fluctuate across economic cycles.

## Practical Usage

```javascript
// Simple PE calculation
function calculatePE(price, eps) {
  if (eps <= 0) return null; // PE is meaningless for negative EPS
  return price / eps;
}

console.log(calculatePE(500, 25)); // 20
```

## Summary

The PE ratio is a useful starting tool, but it doesn't tell the whole story. Always use it alongside other metrics (PB, ROE, Debt/Equity, Dividend Yield) and sector-specific analysis.
